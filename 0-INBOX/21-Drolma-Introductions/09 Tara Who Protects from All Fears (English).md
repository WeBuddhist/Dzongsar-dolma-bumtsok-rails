Tara Who Protects from All Fears
སྒྲོལ་མ་འཇིགས་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྐྱོབ་མ།

**Name** : Tara Who Protects from All Fears
**Mantra** : oṃ tāre tuttāre ture māṃ upakrama rakṣa rakṣa svāhā
**Accomplishment** : Protection from all hosts of fear

Those who wish to be protected from all hosts of fear should rely on Tara Who Protects from All Fears. The ninth of the twenty-one Taras, she is known in several commentaries as the Tara of the Khadira Forest. The root praise honors her through three images — a mudra, a wheel, and rays of light: "With her fingers she adorns her heart with the mudra symbolizing the Three Jewels; adorned with a wheel of every direction without exception, she stirs up the hosts of her own light." She is shown green, with one face and two hands, and it is said that her implements and her light are precisely what reveal her work of protecting from every fear.

Her left hand holds the stem of an utpala flower between thumb and ring finger at her heart, while the index finger, middle finger, and little finger stand upright. This is the mudra symbolizing the Three Jewels, and with these fingers she adorns her heart. Her right hand is in the gesture of supreme giving, and from the wheel adorning her palm, rays of light stream out. Some commentaries explain that the three fingers show the Three Jewels complete within Tara herself; that the utpala held at the heart shows the heart-commitment being carried into one's own mindstream; and that its blossom opening beside the left ear shows one's mindstream being liberated through hearing. Others add that this is Tara's own samaya mudra: when a practitioner forms it according to the ritual, the deity bestows her blessings, and when she is invited with this mudra, she sends out rays of light and actually comes from the buddha-fields of the ten directions. On a hidden level, the Three Jewels are explained as the white essence, the red essence, and the wind-energy gathered into one; binding them at the heart of the channel wheel brings the attainment of inner-heat light pervading every direction.

The meaning of "stirring up" is explained differently across the commentaries. According to Karma Maitri, Ngulchu Dharmabhadra, Gyalwa Gendun Drub, and others, the hosts of her own light streaming from the wheel stir against one another and become so full that they overpower every other light. Tenga Trulku explains that this light clears away the hosts of harmful forces and bestows benefit and happiness on beings. Sangye Nyenpa Rinpoche says that through her stirring or streaming forth, the beings to be tamed are protected from all fears, and Khenpo Tsulnam says that the hosts of the eight fears and the sixteen fears are stirred up or pacified. In the list of sixteen fears given by Sangye Nyenpa — the lion of pride in one's ethics and view, the elephant of ignorance, the fire of hatred, the snake of jealousy, and so on — these fears are named after the inner hosts of afflictions: pride in ethics and view, ignorance, hatred, jealousy, and the rest.

To pray to Tara Who Protects from All Fears, then, is to develop the confidence that through the refuge mudra at her heart and the power of the light radiating in every direction, she protects from all hosts of fear.
