Tara the Great Terror to Every Evil Force
སྒྲོལ་མ་གདོན་རིགས་རྣམ་པར་འཇོམས་པ་འཇིགས་བྱེད་ཆེན་མོ།

Sixth among the twenty-one, this Tara is described as worshipped even by the great gods of the classical Indian pantheon — Indra, Agni, Brahma, the wind-god Vayu, and the divine craftsman Vishvakarman — while hosts of spirits, ghosts, celestial musicians, and nature-spirits all draw near to approach her. It is a striking image: even the powers that other beings fear turn to her for protection, because whatever harm they themselves might cause has already been outmatched by her far greater capacity to pacify it.

Her form reflects that dual role of being both revered and feared in the right measure. She appears wrathful, her expression and bearing carrying the charged intensity needed to meet malevolent forces on their own terms rather than simply avoiding them, since some kinds of harm cannot be dispelled by gentleness alone but must be met and dissolved directly at their source.

Her particular specialty, as her long name announces, is the pacifying of gdon — the subtle, often unseen forms of interference and spirit-harm that Tibetan tradition holds can afflict a person's health, luck, or peace of mind without any visible cause. Where illness has a clear physical origin, medicine is the answer; where suffering arises from causes less easy to name or see, she is the one invoked, precisely because even the gods themselves are said to seek her out when such forces are more than they alone can master.
