Tara Blazing Like Fire
སྒྲོལ་མ་མེ་ལྟར་འབར་བ།

Thirteenth among the twenty-one, she dwells at the center of a garland of light that blazes like the fire said to consume the universe at the end of an aeon — not a small flame but a conflagration of cosmic scale, vast enough to mark the complete and total end of whatever it touches. Standing with one leg extended and the other bent, she uses that same total fire to destroy the hordes of enemies that root verse describes as opposing her.

Beneath the outer image of destruction, the commentators point to a subtler meaning connected to the body's own inner fire — the candali or tummo heat cultivated in advanced meditative practice, said to blaze up the central channel and burn away obstruction from within rather than from without. Read this way, her blazing fire is not only something she wields against outer enemies but something she awakens inside the practitioner, a heat capable of consuming confusion at its very root. Two traditions describe her outer form somewhat differently, though both keep this same core image of fire at the end of an age.

To invoke her is to call on an intensity strong enough to finish what gentler approaches cannot: not a fire that merely warms or illuminates, but one thorough enough to leave nothing of genuine obstruction still standing once it has passed.
