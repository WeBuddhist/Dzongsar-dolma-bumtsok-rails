Tara of Golden Light
སྒྲོལ་མ་གསེར་མདོག་ཅན།

Third among the twenty-one, this Tara is praised in the root verse as gold-blue in color, born from a lotus that opens on water, her hands themselves adorned like lotus blossoms. Where the first two Taras are known chiefly for swiftness and radiance, she is known for completeness: the commentators identify her as the living embodiment of the six perfections that a bodhisattva cultivates over the long path to Buddhahood — generosity, discipline, patience, joyful effort, meditative stability, and wisdom — each one present in her as naturally as color is present in gold.

Her most distinctive feature is simply stated but rich in meaning: she rises from water the way a lotus does, untouched by the mud beneath her, and her hands are described as themselves being like lotuses, so that wherever she reaches to help, that same untouched purity extends outward with her. Some sadhanas elaborate her into a more expansive form with ten arms, each hand carrying a different implement, so that the six perfections she embodies can be shown acting simultaneously in ten directions of need.

To call on her is to call on completeness itself — not one virtue in isolation, but the whole harmonious set of qualities that make a life of genuine benefit to others possible. Where beings are scattered and depleted by only ever managing one virtue at the cost of another, she is invoked as the reminder, and the living proof, that generosity, patience, discipline, effort, stillness, and insight were never meant to be separate achievements at all.
