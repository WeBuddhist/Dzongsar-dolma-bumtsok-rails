# Brilliant Rays of Light: An Explanation of the Praise to Tārā in Twenty-One Homages ^0

## Homage ^I-0

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^I-1]]

Namo Buddhaya! ^I-1

## Outline of the Praise ^II-0

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^II-1]]

This praise has two meanings: the praise itself and the benefits. The first has three parts: praise by way of the sambhogakāya through the form of the body, praise by way of the dharmakāya, and praise by way of enlightened activity. The first of these has three: the presentation of the history by way of the names, the form of the body, and the ornaments and attire. The middle two consist of praise by way of the peaceful form and praise by way of the wrathful form. The first of these has three: praise by way of light rays, by way of hand symbols and sentient beings, and by way of the homage paid by buddhas and bodhisattvas. ^II-1

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^II-2]]

There are three parts to the praise by way of the wrathful form: praise by way of power, praise by way of the great worldly beings, and praise by way of enlightened activity. That has two: praise by way of common enlightened activity and by way of uncommon enlightened activity. There are four parts to the praise by way of ornaments and attire: praise by way of peaceful attire, by way of wrathful attire, by way of common crown ornaments, and by way of wrathful attire. ^II-2

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^II-3]]

The first has two parts: hand symbols and crown ornaments. The subsequent wrathful [section] has three: praise by way of light rays, by way of the posture, and by way of hand symbols. The third general topic, praise by way of enlightened activity, has six: praise by way of secret mantra, by way of power, by way of pacifying poison, by way of removing obstacles, by way of removing epidemics, and by way of removing disharmony. The second general topic, the benefits, has four: the distinction of intention, the distinction of time, the detailed explanation of the actual benefits, and the detailed explanation of the benefits summarized by way of number. Composed by the lay practitioner Drakpa Gyaltsen. ^II-3

## The Actual Praise ^1-0

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-1]]

Oṃ! Homage to the Noble and Venerable Tārā. ^1-1

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-2]]

"Homage to Tārā" means she who liberates from the suffering of cyclic existence. "Swift" means she is swift in the interest of sentient beings. "Heroine" means she is a heroine because she vanquishes the afflictions. "Her eyes are like a momentary lightning bolt" is a metaphor for her luminosity. "Lord of the Three Worlds" refers to Avalokiteśvara, the protector of the three worlds. "From the water-born face" refers to the lotus or utpala flower that arose from his tears. "Born from the opening of its stamens" means Tārā arose from the opening of the lotus. ^1-2

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-3]]

"Homage to her whose face is like the autumn moon" is a metaphor for her white light. "Full" means like the full moon. "A hundred gathered" means her face is like hundreds of full moons piled together. "Blazing with the brilliant light of a thousand stars gathered together" is a metaphor for the immeasurable rays of light radiating from those two. ^1-3

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-4]]

"Homage to her whose golden-blue color is as radiant as the light of refined gold." "Born from the water" refers to the lotus held as a hand-emblem. "Her hands are adorned with lotuses" means she holds a lotus in her left hand. "Generosity" refers to the perfection of generosity. "Diligence" refers to the perfection of diligence. "Austerity" is ethical discipline; "serenity" is the pacification of the afflictions; "patience" refers to the perfection of patience. "Meditative concentration" refers to the perfection of meditative concentration. "She whose sphere of activity is wisdom" refers to wisdom; by practicing these six perfections, the body of Tārā is attained, and by the emblem of that, the utpala is signified. ^1-4

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-5]]

"Homage to the crown protuberance of the Tathāgatas" means she is like the crown protuberance on the heads of all the Tathāgatas. "She who acts with infinite victory" means she acts to be completely victorious over all adversaries, such as Māras and enemies. "She who has attained all the perfections without exception" means she has attained the ten perfections, which are the essence of the ten stages without exception. "Highly honored by the Victors' heirs" means the Victors' heirs are all the bodhisattvas. "Highly honored" means she is carried as their crowning jewel. ^1-5

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-6]]

"Homage to the syllables of TUTTĀRA HŪṂ" means the mantra is TUTTĀRE HŪṂ. "Syllables" refers to the power and light of those mantra syllables. "Filling desire, directions, and space" means filling the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm. "Trampling the seven worlds with her feet" means she tramples the five classes of beings of the desire realm, the form realm as the sixth, and the formless realm as the seventh. "Able to summon without exception" means "without exception" she acts for the benefit of sentient beings. "Able to summon" means she is able to bring those such as kings under her power. ^1-6

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-7]]

"Homage to you who are worshipped by Indra, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and the various Īśvaras" means that Śakra, the lord of the gods, Agni, Brahmā, Vāyu, and their lord, the great Īśvara, offer worship at her feet. "Praised from before by the hosts of bhūtas, vetālas, gandharvas, and yakṣas" means that bhūtas are those such as Gaṇapati; vetālas are those such as the great Īśvara; gandharvas are those such as Pañcaśikha; and yakṣas are those such as Vaiśravaṇa. "By the hosts" means she is praised from before by the eight classes of gods and spirits, such as those. ^1-7

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-8]]

"Homage to you who utterly destroy the magical wheels of adversaries with the sounds TRAḌ and PHAṬ" means that with fierce speech, she destroys and averts the evil applications performed by others. "Right leg bent and left extended" refers to her posture. "Treading with her feet" carries the meaning explained above. "Blazing amidst a swirling mass of fire" means she sits majestically in the midst of a conflagration. ^1-8

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-9]]

"Homage to TURE, the great fearsome one" means that by the fear of TURE, all Māras are dispelled; because she causes the Māras to tremble, she is the "great fearsome one." "Destroyer of the champions of Māra" means that the "champions" are the Māras of the mental afflictions. By destroying them, the other three are destroyed as a consequence. "Her lotus face is marked with frowning wrinkles" means that the "lotus face" is the face of the glorious one, as lovely as a blooming lotus. "Marked with frowning wrinkles" means she abides in a wrathful form. "Slayer of all enemies without exception" means she slays all discordant factors, such as the mental afflictions. ^1-9

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-10]]

"Homage to her whose fingers, in the mudrā symbolizing the Three Jewels, beautifully adorn her heart" means that the thumb and ring finger of her left hand hold a lotus at her heart. The three extended fingers are the sign symbolizing the Three Jewels. "Adorned with wheels in every direction without exception" means the palm of her right hand, which performs the mudrā of supreme generosity, is adorned with a wheel. "With a turbulent mass of her own light" means that light rays stream from the wheel in her hand, overwhelming all other lights. ^1-10

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-11]]

"Homage to her whose crown emits garlands of light, radiant with supreme joy" means that she fulfills the desires of all sentient beings who are filled with supreme joy; "majestic" means overwhelming others. Her crown is made beautiful by garlands of many various jewels. "Laughing, she laughs aloud with the sound of TUTTĀRE" means she sounds the laughter of the mantra. "She who brings Māras and the world under her power" means that through that laughter of the mantra, she brings the Māras and the world under her power. ^1-11

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-12]]

"Homage to her who has the power to summon all the hosts of local guardians" means the ten guardians of the directions. "She who has the power to summon all" means they act as her messengers by obeying whatever she commands. "With the syllable HŪṂ and her quivering frown" means that while she abides in her wrathful form, her heart is marked by the syllable HŪṂ. "She who completely delivers from all destitution" means she liberates all sentient beings who are oppressed by unfavorable conditions and deprived of favorable conditions from their suffering, and establishes them in bliss. ^1-12

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-13]]

"Homage to her whose head is adorned with a crescent moon" means her head is adorned with the moon of the first day of the lunar month. "She whose ornaments all blaze intensely" means that rays of light blaze forth from that moon. "From Amitābha amidst her matted locks" means she is adorned on her head by Amitābha amidst her matted locks. "Always emitting a brilliant light" means that Amitābha radiates immeasurable rays of light for the benefit of sentient beings. ^1-13

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-14]]

"Homage to her who dwells within a garland of blazing fire like the conflagration at the end of the eon" means that at the end of the eon, when seven suns arise, all earth, stones, and mountains burn like fire, and she dwells in the midst of such a conflagration. "With right leg extended and left bent, surrounded by joy" refers to a sitting posture that is the reverse of the previous one. "She who completely destroys the armies of enemies" means she destroys all unfavorable conditions. ^1-14

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-15]]

"Homage to her who strikes the surface of the earth with her palm and tramples it with her feet" means that she strikes the ground with her left hand and performs the threatening mudrā. "With a wrathful frown and the syllable HŪṂ" means that by uttering the syllable HŪṂ, light rays radiate from the vajra held in her right hand, filling the realms of the seven classes of beings and clearing away all their obstacles, thereby "shattering the seven levels." Alternatively, it means she causes the nāgas, asuras, and others who dwell in the seven levels beneath the earth to be scattered into pieces. ^1-15

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-16]]

"Homage to the Blissful, Virtuous, and Peaceful One" means: "Blissful" is she who possesses uncontaminated bliss; "Virtuous" is she who is virtuous because she is free from the afflictions to be abandoned; "Peaceful" is she who acts from within the state of having pacified suffering. "She whose very sphere of experience is the peace of nirvāṇa" means: she is in nirvāṇa because conceptual thoughts have been exhausted, and she acts from within the state of peaceful bliss because suffering has been pacified. "Perfectly endowed with OṂ and SVĀHĀ, she who utterly destroys great evils" means: by the essence of her own body as the dharmakāya, she destroys the afflictions of others—those things which are to be abandoned. ^1-16

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-17]]

"Homage to her who, surrounded by joy, completely shatters the bodies of enemies" refers to the arrangement of the ten-syllable speech: OṂ TĀRE TUTTĀRE TURE SVĀHĀ, which is the peaceful mantra. "From the HŪṂ of intrinsic awareness, she is Tārā herself" refers to OṂ NAMAḤ TĀRE NAMO HARI HŪṂ HARA SVĀHĀ, which is the wrathful mantra. ^1-17

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-18]]

"Homage to her who stamps her feet, the Ture, whose essence is the seed-syllable in the form of HŪṂ" means: the wrathful body is generated from HŪṂ. "Mount Meru, Mandara, and Vindhya" means: outside the three-thousandfold world system, the mountain called Mandara is also pierced by the light rays of the syllable HŪṂ. "She who causes the three worlds to tremble" means: she causes the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm to shake. ^1-18

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-19]]

"Homage to her who holds in her hand the deer-marked one, which has the form of a celestial lake" means: she holds in her hand a moon mandala that is like a celestial lake. That is the sign of clearing away the poison of the mental afflictions. "Deer-marked" refers to the moon. "By pronouncing Tāra twice and the syllable PHAṬ, she clears away poisons without exception" means: by that mantra "Tāra," she clears away the poisons of the stable and the moving. ^1-19

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-20]]

"Homage to the queen of the hosts of gods, whom gods and kinnaras rely upon." These two lines mean that she is worshipped at her feet by the leaders of those divine hosts, such as the kinnara king Druma and others. "With the majesty of her joyful and complete armor" means she is arrayed in the manner of the deity's body and the armor of mantra. "She who dispels disputes and bad dreams" means she clears away the disputes of non-Buddhists and evil dreams. ^1-20

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-21]]

"Homage to her whose two eyes are as brilliant as the full sun and moon; by reciting the two HARA syllables and TUTTĀRE, she dispels the most violent contagious diseases." This means that the fullness of the sun and moon is symbolized by the right eye as the moon and the left eye as the sun; "brilliantly radiant" means that light emanates and shines from those two, the sun and moon. "By reciting the two HARA syllables" means that through the speech of the peaceful and wrathful mantras, she clears away the most violent infectious diseases and so forth. ^1-21

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-22]]

"Homage to her who is established by the three suchnesses" means that the three syllables Oṃ, Āḥ, and Hūṃ are arranged upon her body, speech, and mind. "Perfectly endowed with the power of peace" means she possesses the power to pacify the afflictions. "The hosts of spirits, zombies, and yakṣas" refers to the pacification of all obstacles. "Supreme Ture, the destroyer" means that through the power of the ten-syllable mantra, she destroys all adverse conditions. ^1-22

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^1-23]]

"This praise with the root mantra" refers to this praise of the peaceful and wrathful root mantras, and the "homage" is this very praise in twenty-one verses. ^1-23

## Benefits ^2-0

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^2-1]]

"By anyone endowed with perfect devotion for the Goddess," means: by any intelligent person who recites this with body, speech, and mind for the Goddess. ^2-1

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^2-2]]

"Having risen at dusk and at dawn," means: at dusk, one recollects her wrathful form, and at dawn, one recollects her peaceful form. "Recollection bestows all fearlessness," means: by merely recollecting the deity's form, fearlessness is granted. "All misdeeds are thoroughly pacified," means: by mere recollection. "It destroys all miserable rebirths," means: the results of miserable rebirths are pacified. ^2-2

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^2-3]]

"By seventy million Conquerors," means: they will swiftly and continuously confer empowerment upon me. "And one will attain greatness beyond this," means: not only in this life, but one will proceed to that ultimate state of Buddhahood. ^2-3

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^2-4]]

As for the common results, "Its great and violent poison, whether stationary or moving," means: the great and violent poisons of the stationary and moving, such as the nag po brtsegs pa and poisonous snakes. "Even if eaten or drunk," means: even if one has eaten or drunk poison, one attains complete neutralization through recollection; by recollecting the praise, the deity, and the mantra, the poisons are cleared away. ^2-4

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^2-5]]

"Those afflicted by spirits, epidemics, and poisons," including their causes and effects, means: the masses of suffering are thoroughly abandoned, and those residing within oneself are abandoned through the power of Tārā's rigs sngags. "And for other sentient beings as well," means: just as for oneself, one is able to benefit other sentient beings too. ^2-5

![[bo-རྗེ་བཙུན་གྲགས་པ་རྒྱལ་མཚན།#^2-6]]

"If recited two, three, or seven times," means: because one relies upon the goddess within oneself and before oneself, it must certainly be recited. "Those desiring a child will obtain a child, and those desiring wealth will obtain wealth; all desires will be fulfilled," means: all supreme and common attainments will be accomplished. "Obstacles will be non-existent and individually destroyed," means: because there are no interruptions to the recitation, obstacles are non-existent; those things to be abandoned are individually destroyed by their respective antidotes. ^2-6
